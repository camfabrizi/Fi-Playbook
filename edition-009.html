Just sports… right?

NFL Sundays are back, college football is choosing chaos, and somehow Sydney Sweeney made the sports page.

🏈 First things first

Football season has arrived.

NFL Sundays are officially back, college football has already gone completely off the rails, and I spent my Sunday watching the Panthers and Bears combine for 96 points in person.

So yes, football season has arrived. And if this weekend was any indication, we are in for a very normal and peaceful season (NOT). 🙂

Here’s what you need to know.

Read the original newsletter on Beehiiv →

🏆 What happened this week

Panthers vs. Bears was basically an offensive free-for-all.

The Panthers opened their season at home against the Chicago Bears and lost 59–37.

Before you see 59 points and assume Carolina was terrible across the board, the offense actually gave us something to work with. Bryce Young threw for 361 yards and three touchdowns, and Carolina even tied the game 31–31 in the third quarter.

Then…the defense forgot the game was still happening.

Chicago scored three straight touchdowns, Carolina couldn’t keep up, and what had been a competitive game turned into a blowout pretty quickly.

Why you should care: The Panthers offense showed some serious life. The defense? We may need to circle back.

🤠 College football

Texas pulled off an INSANE comeback.

No. 1 Ohio State looked like it had this one handled. Texas entered the fourth quarter down 23–3.

And then Arch Manning and the Longhorns basically said, actually, no.

Texas scored 21 unanswered points in the fourth quarter and won 24–23, scoring the game-winning touchdown with just 25 seconds left.

Before this game, AP No. 1 teams were 197–0 when holding a 20-point lead in the fourth quarter since 2004. Now they’re 197–1.

Why you should care: Taking down the No. 1 team is already huge. Coming back from 20 points down in the FOURTH QUARTER to do it? That’s the kind of game people will be talking about all season.

🏈 NFL

The Saints almost pulled off the comeback of the weekend.

The Lions looked like they had this one completely under control, going up 21–0 on New Orleans.

Then the Saints decided to make things interesting.

New Orleans came all the way back, tied the game with just over a minute left, and forced overtime. Detroit scored a touchdown first, the Saints answered with one of their own…and instead of kicking the extra point to keep the game going, New Orleans went for two and the win.

They didn’t get it. Detroit escaped 31–30.

Why you should care: Going for two there is basically saying, we are ending this game right now, one way or another. It didn’t work, but I respect the drama.

🎾 Tennis

So close, Ben Shelton.

American Ben Shelton made it all the way to the US Open final, putting him one win away from ending a 23-year Grand Slam drought for American men.

He ultimately fell to Alexander Zverev, who took home his first US Open title.

Meanwhile, Elena Rybakina beat Aryna Sabalenka to win the women’s title and take over the World No. 1 ranking.

Tennis season said goodbye with a little drama, a new No. 1, and Americans getting this close.

🚨 Sports chaos headlines

LSU vs. the entire SEC.

LSU tried to add two players who had already spent time in the NFL back onto its college roster. A Louisiana judge opened the door for them to potentially play, but the SEC said absolutely not.

Things escalated so much that the SEC sued LSU, and conference leaders even discussed the possibility of kicking the school out of the SEC.

Yes. Kicking LSU out of the SEC.

LSU eventually backed down and left the players off the roster, so nobody got kicked out — but college football nearly gave us a courtroom drama before Week 2.

Translation: LSU tested the rules. The SEC basically said, try me.

☕️ What’s the tea?

Sydney Sweeney + sports = internet meltdown.

Okay, so somehow Sydney Sweeney is now part of sports news.

She’s in a new campaign for sports prediction platform Novig, and the whole thing is basically Sydney posing with sports equipment while wearing…very little.

And female athletes were not loving it.

Several Olympians and professional athletes called out the campaign. Some started posting their own training and competition videos with the message: this is what a woman in sports actually looks like.

Sydney seemingly responded by sharing photos from ESPN’s old Body Issue, where athletes famously posed nude.

The tea: Some people see it as a provocative ad that did exactly what it was supposed to do: get people talking. Others think using a nearly naked woman to sell a sports product feels especially tone-deaf when female athletes are still fighting to be recognized for their talent instead of their bodies.

But here’s the thing…whether you loved the ad, hated it, or thought it completely missed the mark: Had you heard of Novig before this?

Exactly. So controversial or not, the marketing definitely did something.

🏟️ Fi’s on-field report

96 points. One very loud stadium.

Yes, the On-Field Report is ACTUALLY on the field this week.

I went to the Panthers home opener against the Bears, and let me tell you: 96 combined points is a lot of touchdowns to witness in person.

The good news? Bank of America Stadium was alive, the Panthers offense actually looked exciting at times, and Bryce Young gave us plenty to cheer about.

When Chuba Hubbard scored to tie it 31–31 in the third quarter, the stadium was LOUD. It genuinely felt like we had a game.

And then Chicago scored three unanswered touchdowns.

So my official Week 1 diagnosis is: Offense = promising. Defense = we need to talk.

But football is back in Charlotte, I got my overpriced stadium drink, and we’re only one game in. I will remain cautiously optimistic. Ask me again next Sunday.

🤯 Sports fun fact

Ever wonder why it’s called a touchdown? In early versions of football, scoring involved actually touching the ball down on the ground after crossing the goal line. The rules eventually changed, but the name stuck.
